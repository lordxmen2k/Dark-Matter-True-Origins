\chapter{Primordial Black Holes}
\label{ch:pbh}

There is a candidate that requires no particle, no symmetry, no coupling and
no production mechanism beyond gravity. Its only problem is that it is not
dark: black holes radiate, accrete and lens, and those three effects have
closed most of the mass range.

\section{What primordial black holes are}Black holes formed in the early universe, before
matter-antimatter annihilation and long before there were stars, would form
a population with a mass function spanning an enormous range --- from
planetary masses to supermassive masses --- determined by the density
fluctuations at the horizon scale at the time of formation. The horizon mass
equals the horizon scale at the time of formation, and the horizon mass
equals the horizon scale at the time of formation, and the horizon mass
equals the horizon scale at the time of formation. The horizon mass equals
the horizon scale at the time of formation.

The mass function is set by the amplitude and the shape of the primordial
power spectrum on the scales that exit the horizon during inflation. This is
the candidate's great attraction: the abundance is not a free parameter but
a prediction from inflation, and the shape of the resulting mass function
has structure --- a peak, a tail, and a series of secondary features --- that
can be searched for.

Carr and Kuhnel's 2018 review is the standard reference for the constraints
and the windows \idx{Carr and Kuhnel}.

\section{Where the windows survive}The constraints are extremely mass-dependent,
because each observational technique is sensitive to a different range.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Below about $10^{-16}\ \Msun$} --- the evaporating range ---
Hawking radiation. The lifetime of a black hole scales as the cube of its
mass, and the evaporation products are gamma rays and particles. The
extragalactic gamma-ray background, the Galactic gamma-ray spectrum, the
antiproton and positron fluxes, and the CMB ionization signature all
constrain this range. The observational windows in it are narrow.
\item \textbf{Around $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-11}\ \Msun$} --- the asteroid and
lunar range --- the constraints come from femtolensing of stars in the
Magellanic Clouds, from the survival of neutron stars in the Galactic
environment against capture by PBHs, and from the evaporation products. This
is where the windows are widest.
\item \textbf{Above about $10^{-11}\ \Msun$} --- the sublunar to stellar
range and upward --- microlensing surveys of wide-field surveys such as
OGLE, EROS, Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam, and the MACHO and EROS collaborations'
limits, together with the dynamical effects on star clusters and dwarf
galaxies. The wide-field surveys have pushed the entire range from roughly
$10^{-11}$ to $10^2\ \Msun$ to a fraction of a per cent of the dark matter.
\item \textbf{Above about $10^2\ \Msun$} --- the range accessible to
accretion and to gravitational waves --- the strongest constraints come from
X-ray binary systems, from the CMB and the 21~cm signal from accreting
black holes, and from the merger rate implied by LIGO and Virgo.
\end{itemize}

The 2025 and 2026 gravitational-wave catalogues have tightened the
sublunar and asteroid windows specifically, because a merger rate in the
asteroidal mass range would be a direct signature \idx{PBH gravitational waves}.
And a 2020 argument introduced a genuinely new consideration: the existence
of ultra-faint, dark-matter-free dwarf galaxies --- ``ghostly galaxies'' ---
is not predicted by standard cosmology at all, and a population of
primordial black holes produces exactly such satellite populations
\idx{Ghostly galaxies}.

\section{The argument for and the argument against}The argument for is
elegant. Black holes require no new particle, no new symmetry, no new
interaction, and no unexplained coupling; the mass function has structure
that can be searched for; and a detection would be unambiguous in a way
that a particle detection is not, because a black hole of a given mass in
the Solar System could only be primordial in any plausible scenario.

The argument against is equally elegant and is about abundance rather than
about physics. The mass function of primordial black holes is determined by
the primordial power spectrum, and the same power spectrum also determines
the CMB and the amplitude of large-scale structure. Over most of the mass
range, the abundance required to constitute all the dark matter is excluded
by something else --- and the exclusions are not marginal.

The most serious objection is the one about the LIGO and Virgo mergers.
Black holes in the stellar mass range can form through direct collapse in
the early universe only if the primordial power spectrum has a feature at
the corresponding scale, and such a feature also raises the amplitude of
the primordial curvature perturbation in a way that is constrained by the
CMB, the Planck spectrum, and the non-observation of a spectral feature in
the CMB.

\section{Decision criteria}A primordial black hole population would be
confirmed by a microlensing event or a gravitational-wave merger in a mass
range where the population cannot be astrophysical, or by an evaporation
signal. It would be excluded in a mass range by a survey that reaches
sufficient statistical depth. The class is the most cleanly falsifiable in
the book: the windows are narrow, the predictions are quantitative, and
the abundance is a prediction rather than a parameter.

\begin{keytakeaways}
\item Primordial black holes require no new particle or interaction, and
their abundance is a prediction of inflation rather than a free parameter.
\item The surviving windows are concentrated in the asteroid and sublunar
mass range, and the 2025--2026 gravitational-wave data have tightened them.
\item The class is the most decisively falsifiable in the book, which makes
it a high-value target even for a low prior.
\item The ghostly-galaxy argument is a real and interesting case for PBH
dominance, and it is not yet a proof of it.
\end{keytakeaways}
