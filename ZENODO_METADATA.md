Title: Dark Matter: Its True Origins, Whether We Have Already Tapped It, and Why the Answer Matters

**Dark matter is 26.8 per cent of the universe's energy density and 84 per cent of its matter. The density is known to two per cent; the particle has never been observed.** Five physically independent handles — galaxy rotation curves, cluster dynamics, gravitational lensing, the acoustic peaks of the cosmic microwave background, and baryon acoustic oscillations in galaxy clustering — converge on the same number, and the Bullet Cluster adds the decisive constraint that the dominant mass component is collisionless and electromagnetically inert. But the abundance is the only thing the gravitational evidence can deliver: it cannot measure the mass, the couplings, the spin, or the identity. This book is an assessment of the field at that boundary as of late 2026. It takes each candidate class in turn — the WIMP, whose miracle reproduced the relic density with no free parameter and whose exclusion now closes the region where that miracle works; the axion, the only candidate whose motivation is a different problem entirely; ultralight bosons and their solitons; primordial black holes and their surviving windows; sterile neutrinos, SIMP, freeze-in, dark sectors and superheavy relics; and modified gravity, which reproduces galactic rotation curves better than a dark halo does and fails on the scales where the dark matter problem actually lives. It audits the live frontier with the standard the field wrote down: the exclusion curves now reach the solar neutrino fog, the practical ceiling of the xenon programme is a factor of a few beyond the current best limit rather than two orders of magnitude, and the one live candidate — a single 248 keV event at 3.4 sigma local and 2.6 sigma global significance, in an analysis that was not blinded — falls short of the five sigma in two independent experiments that a discovery requires. It sets that candidate against the anomaly record of the last five years, in which the 3.5 keV line, the EDGES signal, the 21 cm anomaly and the SARAS 3 result were real measurements that did not replicate, and draws the conclusion that the binding constraint on the field's progress is replication rather than sensitivity.

**Have we already tapped it? The question has five answers, and the interesting ones are positive.** Dark matter has been used as a gravitational probe since 1933, at a precision unmatched by anything the field has attempted with a detector, and the gravitational results are the specification that every candidate must satisfy rather than a preliminary to the real work. The Sun and the planets gravitationally capture a private supply of the stuff, raising the local density by four to five orders of magnitude close to the Sun and producing a directed dark matter wind whose measurement would constrain the halo velocity distribution that every laboratory limit currently assumes. It has, on paper, been harvested: the arithmetic of the captured population is enormous, and the honest conclusion is that the coupling does not exist in nature, which is a decidable statement rather than an open question. Quantum devices respond to the ambient field. The particle does not, and the distance between those two facts is the distance the field has to close. Because the origins question — where a density of 0.12 came from — cannot be settled by any experiment in isolation, and because the mechanism that explained the density for free has been excluded, the book argues that the origins question is downstream of discovery rather than upstream of it, and that the cheapest decisive test is a measurement of the primordial power spectrum and the CMB spectral distortions. It converts the assessment into a programme: a portfolio of differentiated experiments justified four separate ways, per-candidate criteria with kill conditions attached, a statement of what a discovery would and would not prove, and a roadmap to 2040. **The largest single item in humanity's knowledge debt about the universe is a quarter of the universe that is, in the most literal sense, invisible.**
